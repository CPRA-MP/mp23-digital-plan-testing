## 1985–1995 Regional Land Loss and Organized Restoration

Areas of wetland fragmentation and open water are visible across many parts of the coast, reflecting decades of land loss, altered water movement, storm impacts, and landscape modification. As the scale of land loss became clearer, coastal change was increasingly understood as a regional issue rather than a collection of isolated problems.

Earlier conservation, management, and project efforts helped inform the response, while the late 1980s and 1990s brought more dedicated funding, planning, and implementation capacity to coastal restoration. New programs and plans focused on using sediment, freshwater, and basin-scale strategies to slow or reduce wetland loss.

The 1993 Louisiana Coastal Wetlands Restoration Plan called for a broader approach that included diversions, barrier island rebuilding, beneficial use of dredged material, and changes to hydrologic management.
