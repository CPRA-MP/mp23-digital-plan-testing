A page's startFrame is the frame of its *first* storyboard card. Several pages hold more
than one card — their later cards (f15, f43–f53, f58) are intermediate beats the storyboard
gives a frame but this file doesn't split a page for. The card at f63 straddles a page

the frame numbers are what's used here.

The two kinds of frame props work differently. StoryLabel windows are exact: VideoStory
toggles a label purely off `video.currentTime * fps`, so a label appears at the frame named
here no matter how the pages above it lay out. StoryPage's startFrame/endFrame instead set
the page's height, in direct proportion to the frame span — that proportionality is the
only reason a card arrives near its own video frame, so a page whose frames don't match the
storyboard doesn't just pace oddly, it drags every card after it off its frame.

What still isn't frame-mapped: the title page and the call-to-action page both take a
fixed length. (The storyboard does give the title page f0 and the call to action f131, but
both render outside the framed-page flow.) Each one both pushes the cards after it later
and (since the video scrubs across the container's full height) pulls every card in the
story slightly earlier, so expect some residual drift until they're given frames or
dropped.

StoryLabel is placed as a sibling of StoryPage, not nested inside it — StoryPage's sticky
content box is translated with a CSS transform, which would turn it into the containing
block for a nested StoryLabel's fixed positioning and break its viewport-relative
placement. */}
