## Major Flood Control and River Management

However, the 1927 Mississippi River Flood, which displaced nearly 1 million people, marked a major shift in thinking—from learning how to live with water to focusing on how to control the river. After months of flooding, widespread levee failures, and catastrophic impacts across seven states, Congress authorized the Flood Control Act of 1928 and the Mississippi River and Tributaries Project, resulting in a system of levees and related infrastructure that helped reduce river flood risk, but also blocked the flow of nourishing sediment from the river to the surrounding landscape.

These and other changes have reshaped the coast in increasingly visible ways. It is likely, given the magnitude of change, that parts of Louisiana’s coast today would be largely unrecognizable to those living and working in the region as recently as 100 years ago.
